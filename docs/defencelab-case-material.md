# Case document — what to take from it and where it goes

Source: "Six Real Situations" — cleared for prospects, clients anonymised.

---

## Use it as the source of recognition copy

The document's own table already does the job my drafts were approximating, in the client's voice rather than a copywriter's. These lines should replace the invented recognition details in the hero cards and on the service pages.

| Their line | Belongs to |
|---|---|
| "Our network grew as the company grew. Nobody ever designed it." | **Gap** card |
| "We know we should separate things, but last time we tried, printing broke." | Protect / segmentation |
| "Someone left in March and I am not certain their access is gone." | **Gap** card — strongest single line in the set |
| "Our guest Wi-Fi would not survive a data-protection review." | **Pressure** card |
| "If a firewall started failing, would we know before users complained?" | Detect / Recover |
| "Every new office means starting again from scratch." | Gap / Protect |

The line about someone leaving in March is the one to put in front of a reader first. It's specific, it's ordinary, and almost nobody can answer it confidently.

## Cases mapped to phases

| Case | Primary phase | Also |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Whole company on a shop-bought router | Protect | Identify, Detect |
| 2 — Segmentation nobody notices | Protect | — |
| 3 — Wi-Fi that knows who you are | Protect | Govern |
| 4 — Guest Wi-Fi that survives an audit | Govern | Protect |
| 5 — The quiet month when nothing happened | **Recover** | Detect |
| 6 — One console, every site | Protect | Govern |

**Recover is no longer empty.** Case 5 is resilience and continuity work with a measured outcome — a redundant pair genuinely synchronised, degradation caught as a trend, automation built around a known fault. That is Recover, and it's evidence rather than a claim.

**Govern gains real content** through case 4 (consent, stated purpose, defined retention, attributable sessions) and case 6 (documentation written to be usable by a different provider).

**Respond remains empty.** Six cleared cases, none about an incident. Combined with the empty Respond column in the service menus, that's two independent signals pointing the same way.

`[Ask: are there incident cases that can't be shared, or does incident work not usually become a documented project?]`

## The gap between the cases and the menu

The documented work is network and infrastructure security: firewalls, segmentation, wireless identity, guest portals, switching, central management.

The service menus also advertise DevShield, DevSecOps Services, Product security and Penetration Testing — application-side work that none of the six cases demonstrate.

`[Ask: is there app-sec casework that can be cleared? If not, those menu items are claims without evidence, and the strongest part of the site — the proof — won't cover them.]`

## Two blocks that are already written

### "How we work" → the missing purchase block

The document's closing section covers staged delivery around the business, testing failover rather than asserting it, monitoring that reaches a person with context, and handover with documentation and a runbook. That is the engagement model the site is missing.

Still needed: price entry point, minimum term, and what falls outside scope.

### Trust & confidentiality → already differentiated

Read-only administrative accounts for the client's own IT and security leads. Documentation written for a new hire or a different provider. Administrative access restricted to approved networks. The explicit position that if the client leaves, they aren't held hostage by what only Defence Lab knows.

This addresses the central objection to outsourcing security, and it's a position most competitors won't match. It should be a named block, not a paragraph in About us.

## Facts safe to quote on the site

All from the cleared document, so no confirmation needed:

- twelve network segments, separated and still separated months later
- twenty-four switches and two dozen access points under one console
- exit infrastructure operated in three countries
- guest consent in two languages, 30-day retention, 7-day device access
- a firewall degrading to 88 percent memory with no user disconnected
- 1,310 active connections and 380 Mbps carried through a failover

The scanning detail from case 2 — two non-standard ports identified and opened, rather than a broad rule — is the single most persuasive paragraph in the document for a technical buyer. It demonstrates the difference between separating a network and separating one that people can still work on.

## One tension worth resolving before the copy is finalised

All six cases are Gap and Pressure situations: outgrown infrastructure, a compliance review, planned expansion. None is a crisis.

The team's own description of their audience says clients arrive after an incident.

These can both be true — incident work may be unshareable, or may not produce documented projects — but the site can't be built on both assumptions at once. Whichever is the real pattern determines which card leads and where the proof sits.
