Fifteen named offerings. Seven sit in Protect. Two phases are empty.

### Compliance sits in Govern, and the three items aren't the same kind of thing

- **ISO 27001** — certifiable by an accredited certification body. A certificate exists.
- **SOC 2** — not a certification. An attestation report issued by a licensed CPA firm; Type I covers a point in time, Type II a period of observation.
- **GDPR** — law. No certification exists; compliance is continuous and demonstrated through documentation and process.

**Consequence for the copy.** For both ISO 27001 and SOC 2, auditor independence means the party that prepares you cannot be the party that certifies or attests. Defence Lab's role is readiness, implementation and evidence — the certificate or report comes from a third party. Saying anything that implies otherwise will be caught by exactly the enterprise buyers this offering targets.

This also answers the open bracket on the Pressure card: the in-house/advisory split follows from the framework's own rules. Only the timelines still need confirming.

### AML and MiCA appear nowhere in the offering

The consultant's blog plan leads with a GDPR/AML/MiCA digest. AML and MiCA are financial regulation, not security frameworks, and nothing in the current offering covers them.

`[Ask directly: is there a fintech/crypto direction the blog plan is anticipating, or were these written in by habit? The answer changes both the rubrics and the positioning.]`

## Three problems this exposes

### 1 · No offering for the confirmed demand

Respond and Recover are empty. There is no incident response service, no forensics, no retainer, no recovery — while the team reports that incidents are the main way clients arrive.

Whatever is actually delivered during an incident today exists as work but not as a purchasable thing on the site. It needs a name, a page, and a phone number.

`[Ask the team: what is actually sold and delivered during an incident, and does it have a name internally?]`

### 2 · "Security Health Check" is both a paid service and the free hero button

The hero currently offers `free security health check`, which is the name of a paid Identify service. A visitor who takes the free one reasonably concludes he has had the service.

Fix: the free tool becomes **Coverage check** — eight questions, tells him which phases have an owner. It ends by pointing at the paid Security Health Check, which looks at the systems themselves. Two different things, two different names, one leading to the other.

### 3 · Solutions and Products don't divide cleanly

- `Cybersecurity Awareness` (Solutions) and `Sec awareness` (Products) are the same offering listed twice under different names.
- `DevShield`, `DevSecOps Services` and `Product security` overlap heavily — from outside it isn't clear what distinguishes them or which one to ask about.
- `PhishGuard`, `DevShield` and `Managed Security Gate` are branded names; the rest are descriptive. If Products means "our own branded packages", awareness breaks the rule by appearing in both.

**Revised recommendation.** Earlier I argued for keeping Solutions and Products separate. The actual lists show the split isn't holding, so that argument doesn't survive contact with the data — the consultant is right that the current two-menu structure is a problem. But merging them into one accordion hides the duplication rather than resolving it.

Cut it this way instead:

- **Solutions** — three entries only: Crisis, Gap, Pressure. Situations, not services.
- **Services** — the twelve offerings grouped under their phase, duplicates merged, overlapping app-sec offerings resolved into one or clearly differentiated.
- Branded names (PhishGuard, DevShield, Managed Security Gate) live inside their phase as named products, not as a separate menu.

`Products` as a top-level menu item disappears.
