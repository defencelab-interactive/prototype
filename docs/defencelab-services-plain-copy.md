# "What we actually do" — plain-language rewrite

Method: the service name stays exactly as it is, because buyers search for those terms and technical readers need them. What changes is everything around the name — a plain line beneath it, a description without jargon, and tags a non-specialist can read.

`[brackets]` = unconfirmed fact.

---

## Section intro

Current: *Six engagements that connect — from a one-time baseline to a continuous, owned security function.*

Plain: **Six ways to work with us — from a one-off look at where you stand, to us running security for you.**

The heading claims the six connect, but nothing shows it. Three small labels above the cards would, without a diagram:

## 01 · security posture assessment
**Find out where you actually stand**

We look at what you have and tell you which gaps matter and which don't. You get a short list in the order things should be fixed — not a 200-page report nobody reads.

- what's actually exposed right now
- what a customer's security questionnaire would ask you
- what to fix first, in order

*Was: risk-rated gaps · framework mapping · prioritized roadmap*

---

## 02 · devshield — managed security program
**Security that has an owner every month, not just before an audit**

Instead of a project that ends, security becomes a standing part of how you operate: a monthly list of what's being fixed, someone whose job it is to close it, and a report you can put in front of your board.

- a monthly plan with names against it
- fixes that get closed, not just logged
- a summary your board can read without translation

*Was: monthly priorities & backlog · hardening cycles · executive reporting*

---

## 03 · mdr / mxdr — 24/7 detection & response
**Someone is watching, and wakes up when it matters**

Most companies already collect the evidence of a break-in and nobody reads it. We watch it around the clock, and when something is wrong we act on it — cutting off the access rather than sending you an alert to deal with on Monday.

- watched day and night, including weekends
- we shut the intruder out, not just tell you about them
- we start within `[X hours]` of something being spotted

*Was: 24/7 coverage · threat detection & containment · sla-backed response*

> The 24/7 claim and the response time both need confirming before publication. They're the two facts a buyer will test first.

---

## 04 · vciso (fractional)
**A security lead without hiring one**

You get the person who decides what matters first, answers the hard questions from customers and investors, and keeps the written record of what was decided and why — a few days a month instead of a full-time salary.

- decides what to do first, and what to leave
- answers the security questions customers and investors ask
- keeps the written record an auditor will want

*Was: strategy & governance · risk management · program ownership*

---

## 05 · penetration testing
**We try to break in, on purpose**

Real attempts against your apps, your cloud and your network — the same way someone hostile would. Afterwards you get a list of what actually worked, ordered by what an attacker would use first, and we come back to check your fixes held.

- we attack it the way an outsider would
- findings written so your engineers can act on them
- we re-test after you've fixed things

*Was: app, cloud, and infra testing · actionable findings · retest support*

---

## 06 · human risk management
**The part where somebody clicks the link**

Most break-ins start with a person, not a server. We send real phishing to find out who's vulnerable, train the people who fall for it, and show you whether the risk is actually going down over time.

- real phishing attempts, not a quiz
- training that goes to whoever needs it, when they need it
- proof the risk is dropping, not just that training happened

*Was: phishing simulations · training programs · behavior analytics*

## Rules applied

- **Names untouched.** `mdr / mxdr`, `vciso`, `devshield` all stay. Searchable, and a technical buyer needs them.
- **Plain line under each name.** Written as the reader would describe the problem to a colleague, not as the industry describes the service.
- **No terms without a plain equivalent alongside.** *Posture*, *hardening*, *containment*, *behavior analytics* and *framework mapping* are gone from the reader-facing text.
- **Verbs, not nouns.** "We shut the intruder out" rather than "threat containment". Nouns hide who does what.
- **What you get, not what we perform.** Every tag names an outcome the reader can recognise.
- **Contrast where it's honest.** "Not a 200-page report", "not just an alert on Monday", "not just that training happened" — each names the disappointing version the reader has probably already bought once.
